# Feynman Skill

A small AI skill that teaches you anything one concept at a time, the way Richard Feynman might have inspired you to learn: you don't understand it until you can teach it back with the source closed.

Point it at a file, a link, a repo, some pasted text, or just a concept. It builds you a ladder, teaches one rung, then makes you explain it back in your own words. When you go vague, it finds the exact word where you went vague and patches only that. Nothing wasted re-teaching what you already own.

This is the real file I use, cleaned up so it runs for anyone. Fork it, make it yours, break it, rebuild it. That is the point.

## What makes it different

Most "learning" prompts dump a summary and call it a day. You read it, feel smart for an hour, and retain nothing. This one is built on the opposite bet.

- **It hunts the seam, not the whole topic.** The only place learning is still owed is the exact spot your explanation goes hand-wavy. The loop spends all its effort finding that spot and filling only it. Vagueness is a coordinate, not a style note.
- **It reorders the material by dependency, not by page.** The load-bearing idea, the one everything else is a consequence of, gets taught first. Walking a document top to bottom is easy. Re-sequencing it so it actually builds is the work.
- **It refuses to let you parrot.** Repeat its words back and it rejects the answer. You have to re-frame it in your own analogy. Re-framing is the proof you own it. Repetition is not.
- **Learning and making are the same motion.** Every concept you fully own gets captured, with your permission, as a reusable "seed" for a post, a script, an essay. The explanation you give when you truly understand something IS the content. By the time you finish, you have both the knowledge and the raw material to teach it to others.
- **The gates are yours.** Capture, read, proceed. Every step is a plain yes or no that you control. The machine is forbidden from deciding for you. Friction is the feature.

It is annotated on purpose. Every section carries a short "Design note" explaining why it exists, so you can see the thinking and fork it with intent instead of copying it blind.

## How to use it

1. Drop the skill file into your AI tool wherever it reads skills or custom instructions.
2. Point it at something you want to learn: `feynman <a file, a URL, pasted text, or a concept>`.
3. Climb the ladder. One rung per turn. Teach each rung back in your own words until it is solid, then move up.

By default it writes a resumable note to a `./feynman/` folder so you can stop mid-ladder and pick it up days later. Change that folder to wherever you keep notes.

## Make it yours

This is a starter, not a finished product, and definitely not something you file requests with.

- **Fork it** and change anything. Add rungs, cut gates, swap the output format, point it at your own voice guide. The license lets you do whatever you want.
- **Show your fork** in Discussions. That is the room. Post what you built, how you bent it, what broke. I read it and I signal-boost the best ones.
- **I don't merge into the core.** No pull-request queue, no bug tracker. The core stays small and opinionated on purpose. Your version lives in your fork, exactly as it should.

Think of me as a curator here, not a maintainer. The gems are the forks.

## Why this is free

I want the people who aren't familiar with this stuff to just try it. One of my missions is to increase exposure, because a lot of folks are feeling like they are being left behind. I was this way too, until AI. Giving away one well-made starter is the fastest on-ramp I know.
